How to use DuoVoice Studio

A complete guide to scripts, voices, emotion, pauses, subtitles, music and video.

1. Write your script

Every line starts with the speaker's name, followed by a colon. The studio supports exactly two speakers, called Host and Guest by default. A line without a name continues the previous speaker's turn, so you can write a long answer across several lines.

Host: Welcome back to the show. Today we're talking about sleep.
Guest (excited): Thanks for having me! It's a stranger story than most people expect.
Host (curious): Let's start simple. What happens to the body at night?
Guest: Quite a lot. {pause 0.6} {thoughtful} The brain replays the day and files memories away.

2. Rename your speakers

Type any name in the two speaker boxes (for example Nora and Jack) and use those same names in the script. Labels are matched to the names you set; Speaker 1 and Speaker 2 also always work. If your script contains a third label, the studio stops and tells you, because only two voices are available.

3. Things that are not spoken

  • Anything in [square brackets], such as [laughs] or [music fades in], is skipped. Use it for notes to yourself.
  • Markdown symbols such as *, # and ` are removed before the text is read.

4. Choose a voice for each speaker

Use the language filter, then pick a voice for each speaker. Mixing a male and a female voice (or a deeper and a lighter one) is the easiest way to make two speakers easy to tell apart. The Speed and Pitch sliders let you fine-tune each voice; small changes (a few percent) sound most natural.

5. Add emotion

Put an emotion in parentheses after a name to apply it to the whole turn, or switch mid-turn with a tag in curly braces:

Guest (sad): It was a hard year.
Host: I'm sorry to hear that. {pause 1.0} {calm} Take your time.

Available emotions: neutral, happy, excited, curious, surprised, serious, thoughtful, calm, sad. A few synonyms also work (cheerful, enthusiastic, shocked, gentle, reflective). Be aware of what these are: each one adjusts speaking speed, pitch and volume. They suggest a mood; they are not a full acting performance. The Emotion strength slider scales the effect (0 turns it off, 2 doubles it), so generate a short test first.

6. Pauses and pacing

Use {pause} for a short beat or {pause 1.5} for a specific length in seconds (maximum 10). The Pause between turns slider sets the default gap when speakers swap, and Natural pause variation adds a tiny random difference so the rhythm does not feel mechanical.

7. Subtitles

Tick Also create subtitles (.srt) before generating. You get a timed subtitle file you can upload to YouTube or any video editor. Subtitles must be enabled at generation time if you later want captions burned into a video.

8. Background music

Pick one of the built-in loops and set its volume. The music fades in and out and is looped or trimmed to match the length of your episode. A level around −18 dB keeps speech clear; go lower (−24 dB) for a subtler bed.

9. Turn the episode into a video

When the audio is ready, a video panel appears. Upload one image to show it for the whole episode, or several images for a slideshow where each picture gets an equal share of the running time. Choose MP4 (works everywhere, ideal for YouTube) or WebM, and optionally burn the captions into the picture. Landscape 16:9 images look best because the video is rendered at 1280×720; other shapes are scaled to fit with bars at the sides.

10. Limits and troubleshooting

  • Script length and rate limits: each script has a maximum length, and each visitor can generate a limited number of episodes per hour, one at a time. The exact numbers are shown above the script box.
  • Files are temporary: generated files are deleted about two hours after creation, so download what you need.
  • A line was treated as a speaker name: a short phrase followed by a colon at the start of a line (like Note: or Tip:) is read as a speaker label. Rephrase it, or put it in [brackets].
  • "The voice service refused the request": the studio relies on a third-party voice service that sometimes rejects or throttles requests. Wait a minute and try again, ideally with a shorter script.
