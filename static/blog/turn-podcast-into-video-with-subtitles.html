How to Turn a Podcast Episode Into a Video With Subtitles

Published October 13, 2026 · 6 min read

Video platforms reward video, not audio files. If you publish podcast-style content, turning each episode into a simple video with images and captions lets you reach people on YouTube and social platforms without filming anything. Here is a practical workflow and the decisions that matter.

Step 1: Generate the audio with subtitles enabled

In DuoVoice Studio, tick Also create subtitles (.srt) before you generate. The subtitle file contains every line with start and end times that match the audio exactly. Decide now, because captions can only be burned into the video if the subtitle file was produced together with the audio.

Step 2: Choose your images

  • One image works well for short episodes or a branded cover: your logo, the episode title, a mascot.
  • Several images create a slideshow, and each image is shown for an equal share of the running time. For a six-minute episode, twelve images means a change about every thirty seconds, which feels natural.
  • Use landscape 16:9 images (1280×720 or larger). Portrait or square images are scaled to fit and will have empty bars at the sides.
  • Keep important content away from the bottom fifth of the picture so that burned-in captions do not cover it.
  • Use images you created or have the right to use.

Step 3: Burned-in captions or a separate subtitle file?

This is the main decision, and each choice has trade-offs:

  • Burned-in captions are part of the picture. They always show, on every platform and player, which is ideal for short clips played with the sound off. The downside is that viewers cannot turn them off and you cannot translate them later without re-rendering the video.
  • A separate .srt file can be uploaded to YouTube alongside the video. Viewers can switch captions on and off, change the language if you add translations, and the text can help the platform understand your content.

Many creators do both for different uses: a burned-in version for social clips and a clean video with an uploaded subtitle file for the main upload.

Step 4: Export

When the audio finishes, open the Turn into video panel, upload your images, pick MP4 (the safest choice for YouTube) and tick Burn in captions if you want them. Rendering usually takes from under a minute to a few minutes depending on the length of the episode.

Step 5: Check before you publish

  • Watch the whole video once. Check pronunciation of names and numbers; synthetic voices occasionally get them wrong.
  • Confirm the captions are readable on a phone screen.
  • Download your files promptly. The studio keeps them for about two hours only.
  • Tell your audience when the voices are computer-generated. It builds trust and some platforms ask for it.

Accessibility is a real benefit

Captions help viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing, people watching in noisy places, and anyone learning the language of your video. Adding them is not just a growth tactic; it makes the episode usable by more people.
